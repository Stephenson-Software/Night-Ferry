# The story, plainly

**Spoilers for the whole game.** In the game this is told in pieces — by the passengers, in the purser's ledgers, behind doors, at the endings — and the notebook's "What is happening to you" page assembles the pieces you have found. This is the assembled version.

## Where you are

The MV *Kittiwake*, the Halde crossing: ten hours of open water from Brekka on the mainland to the island of Halde, away at eight in the evening, alongside at six. It is a Friday.

You are the night steward, on your first crossing. You were taken on this afternoon because the regular steward broke her wrist. **Oskar Brune**, the purser, gave you a jacket, a master key, and one rule: cabin 6 is not to be opened. Cabin 6 was booked three weeks ago at the Brekka office, one berth, cash, in the name of **M. Sollid**, and nobody has come aboard to claim it.

## Who is aboard

- **Hanne Sollid** (46), the woman holding two tickets. She is taking her mother home: **Maren Sollid**'s ashes are in the canvas bag at her feet. The second ticket is for Maren, who always paid her own way. The funeral is tomorrow, beside her father Arne, under a stone with Maren's name already cut. But Maren's note says *not beside Arne — the water off the Halde light, from the boat, at first light*, and Hanne doesn't understand it.
- **Dr Aurel Fenn** (79), Halde's retired doctor. For nineteen years he wrote Maren a monthly referral to a "specialist" in Brekka. There was nothing wrong with her. He never asked where she went; she came home happier than she left.
- **Leopold Raske** (52), the ferry company's man, auditing the books before the sale. He has found nineteen years of cash bookings for cabin 6 kept off the system in the purser's hand, and thinks the purser has been skimming. His report goes in at the quay. He grew up on Halde; Maren taught him long division. The night crossing ends in March and the *Kittiwake* is being sold.
- **Gus Tamm** (55), a lorry driver with a bad back. In his lorry is Maren's piano, sold in the house clearance and bought for cash by a woman who gave no name, for delivery to **Harbour House** — the captain's house.
- **Jory Pell** (19), Maren's last piano pupil, at the stern rail. He left the conservatory she got him into, and hasn't told his parents, who are meeting the boat. Before sailing he saw the captain come down to cabin 6, put her hand flat on the door, and go away without opening it.
- **Tove Ness** (31), the hospice nurse who sat with Maren as she died. She carries a sealed letter: *For I.H. — on the Friday boat.* She doesn't know who I.H. is.
- **Captain Ingrid Halvard** (64), master of the *Kittiwake* for twenty-two years. She always keeps the Friday night watch.
- **Oskar Brune** (58), the purser, who knows everything and says nothing.

## Who cabin 6 was for

**Maren Sollid.** From the spring after Arne died in 2006, Maren crossed to Brekka once a month and came home on the Friday night boat, in cabin 6, and when the mate took the watch at midnight, Ingrid Halvard went down to her. They did the crossword. Maren talked about her pupils; Ingrid talked about the weather. At five Ingrid went back up to bring the boat in. It went on for nineteen years. The specialist was the crossing. Oskar wrote the bookings in his own hand so they would never be in a computer, and every krone went into the till.

Maren died in August. Three weeks ago Ingrid gave Oskar cash and asked him to book cabin 6 in Maren's name, so that Maren could come home in it once more. Then Hanne booked two deck tickets, and Ingrid could not make herself go down the stairs to where Maren actually was.

Maren's letter to Ingrid says: *If H. is on the boat, tell her. I never found the way to, and you were always braver on the water than I was. Tell her I was happy, and on which days. Then the light, if she will let you. Not beside Arne: he was a good man and he would understand, and it was never him I crossed for.*

## How it comes out

By any of several roads: the ledgers (under the purser's hatch while he walks the boat, or from Raske), Jory on the open deck, the piano on the car deck, the inside of cabin 6, the letter. The captain will tell it if you bring her the door, the cabin or the house; the purser will tell it if you bring him the ledgers and the schoolteacher.

**If nobody has told Hanne by four, and you haven't promised the captain to keep it, Ingrid comes down to cabin 6 herself at four in the morning, and Hanne is in the corridor.** It comes out there. Tove hears through the door of cabin 4 and gives Ingrid the letter, and Ingrid reads it aloud to Hanne. A player who never asked anyone anything still hears all of it before the boat docks.

## The choices

Nothing forgets them. *X will remember that.*

- **Ingrid:** "I'll keep it" or "Hanne should know." Kept, she stays on the bridge at four, and if you dare, you can carry Hanne's bag to cabin 6 for an hour while Hanne sleeps, so Maren has her last crossing. Tell Hanne after promising, and Ingrid remembers that too.
- **Hanne:** once she knows — the light, or the churchyard.
- **Dr Fenn:** tell Hanne about the referrals tonight, or let it lie. If he tells her, she knows something good before she knows what, and every way she hears the rest is gentler.
- **Raske:** tell him the bookings were the captain's (it clears Oskar and puts Ingrid's name in front of the board before the sale) or say you know nothing (Oskar is suspended and Ingrid's name stays out).
- **Gus:** let him sleep in cabin 6, or don't. If you do, the captain finds him there at four.
- **Jory:** tell your parents on the quay, or after Saturday.
- **Tove:** give me the letter, or come up with me and give it yourself. If you carry it, you can read it first; Ingrid will see the flap was lifted.
- **Oskar:** open cabin 6 — for yourself or for Gus — and he won't keep you on.

## The endings

1. **Off the light.** Hanne knows, chooses the light, and the captain agrees to stop. At five the *Kittiwake* stops her engines off the Halde light and Hanne and Ingrid put Maren into the water together. Costs: Hanne has a funeral tomorrow at an empty grave; Ingrid stopped her ship with the company's man aboard.
2. **Beside Arne.** Hanne knows but chooses the churchyard — or wants the light and nobody asks the captain to stop. Maren goes in beside Arne, knowingly.
3. **At the door.** Nobody said it, so at four the captain came down. It came out in a corridor, raw. (The light can still be reached after it, if you go to Hanne and then the captain before five.)
4. **The kept crossing.** You promised the captain. Hanne goes ashore not knowing her mother was happy, or where. If you carried the bag to cabin 6, Maren had her hour.

Every last page says what happened, who cabin 6 was for, what you did, and then what it cost — Hanne, Ingrid, Oskar, Raske, Dr Fenn, Jory, Gus, Tove, and you — each in a sentence of their own.

# The last night boat (the second crossing)

**Spoilers for the second crossing.** It is sailed from the first crossing's last screen, in the same save, and everything October left behind is remembered (`lastboat/carried.py`).

## Why a second crossing, and not more passengers on the first

The first crossing is a closed room: one cabin, one question, eight people who each hold part of the answer, and an ending page that pays off every remembered choice. More passengers on that night would dilute the question rather than deepen it, and would give nothing new to the players who have already finished it. A second crossing gives the remembered choices the longest possible reach — what you told Raske in October decides who is in command in March — and lets finished saves sail on. And Maren's letter left one thing undone on every ending: *tell her I was happy, and on which days.* Ingrid only ever told Hanne "every month". The second crossing is about the days.

## Where you are

March, five months later. The MV *Kittiwake*'s last night crossing: Halde to Brekka, away at eight, alongside at six. It is not a Friday. On Monday the buyer's crew takes her south and Halde gets a day boat. You are the steward again — or, if you opened cabin 6 in October, Oskar rang you anyway: "This isn't keeping you on. It's one night."

Cabin 6 is booked, cash, in the name of **I. Halvard**. The captain came up the ramp at seven as a passenger, with a suitcase and a cardboard box tied with string, and the one rule tonight is hers: nobody disturbs six.

## Who is aboard

- **Ingrid Halvard** (65), a passenger. Until midnight she stands at the back of the wheelhouse; from midnight she is in six; from five she is at the stern. She has sold Harbour House and Maren's piano, booked a single south, and in the box are the crossword books from six's drawer — nineteen years of Fridays in both their hands, and in the margins, in Maren's print, *Happy. — M.* At five, the hour she always went back up, she means to put them over the side.
- **Per Aasen** (58), the mate, master for one night. For nineteen years he took the midnight watch on Fridays and wrote *Master below* in the log. He knew by the second month and never said, because she would have stopped if she had known anyone knew. In the night book, in her hand: *0500 — STOP ENGINES, TWO MINUTES. I.H.* She told him it was ballast.
- **Hanne Sollid**, crossing back to Brekka alone, with her mother's diaries: one small pencil cross on one Friday a month, two hundred and twenty-six of them, and nothing else on those pages.
- **Oskar Brune**, purser on his last night. His ledgers go to the buyer on Monday; the cabin-6 column he means to tear out and burn in the galley stove.
- **Gus Tamm**, hauling Maren's piano for the third time: back to the Brekka saleroom, consigned by I. Halvard, "any price".
- **Jory Pell** (20), going back to Brekka to re-audition on Monday with the Grieg. He has not touched a piano since August.

## What October changes

- **Who has the ship.** If you told Raske the truth, the board retired Ingrid in January. If October ended off the light, the company wrote her a letter and she later gave Per the last crossing. Otherwise she asked the company to give it to him.
- **Oskar.** Cleared by Raske's report, or suspended at the quay, cleared of nothing by a January inquiry, and given the last month back.
- **Hanne.** Every October ending but the kept crossing leaves her knowing where her mother went. After the kept crossing she does not, and the crosses mean nothing to her — you can tell her (if you learned it in October, or once you know where the captain is going). If you carried Maren's letter in October and never delivered it, it is still in your pocket, and you can give it to Hanne at last.
- **Everyone's last line**, and a FROM OCTOBER paragraph for Fenn, Tove and Raske, who are not aboard.

## The choices

- **Oskar:** give me the column, or burn it.
- **Jory:** play her piano tonight, or save it for Monday. **Gus:** open the back for Jory (you answer for it), or keep it strapped. If both say yes, after midnight you can fetch Jory down to the car deck; he plays the Grieg, and if the captain is in six above, she hears it.
- **Per:** tell her you knew, or let her go thinking nobody did. If he tells her, it is said at the change of watch at midnight.
- **Ingrid:** give the books to Hanne, or "do what you came to do". She agrees to give them only if you know about Hanne's crosses, or she heard the piano; otherwise she says Hanne has never asked her for anything.
- **Hanne:** once she knows, and you know what is in the box, and Ingrid is in six — go and knock, or leave her tonight. If she knocks, Ingrid gives her the books in six and reads her which days.
- **Opening six** before midnight is the captain's one rule; Oskar notices.

## Five o'clock, and the endings

**Five o'clock is the failure-proof answer.** If the books are not already Hanne's, Per sends for you. At the stern rail Ingrid tells you all of it and shows you one margin — *Happy. — M.* — and, unless you already told her to do what she came to do, asks: give them to Hanne (works only if you know the crosses or she heard the piano), take them with you, or let them go.

1. **On which days.** The books reach Hanne — because she knocked on six, or because you talked Ingrid round and brought Hanne to the stern at five, or at the rail. Ingrid reads her which Fridays were happy. Ingrid still takes the train south, with Hanne's address.
2. **The column.** The books go over the side, but you gave Hanne Oskar's column: the dates, in a purser's hand, which match her crosses.
3. **Over the side.** The books go, and Hanne has only the crosses.
4. **Kept from the record.** You told Ingrid to take them with her; they go south in a box under a spare bed.
